03The reactionAluminum in. Air in. Electricity out.

An aluminum plate oxidizes at the anode and gives up electrons. Oxygen from ambient air is reduced at the cathode and takes them back. The electrolyte carries hydroxide between the two. The electrons take the long way around, through your load.

AnodeAl + 3OH⁻ → Al(OH)₃ + 3e⁻E° = −2.31 V
CathodeO₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻E° = +0.40 V
Overall4Al + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Al(OH)₃E° = +2.71 V

Practical cell voltage under load is 1.0–1.2 V. Cells are stacked in series to reach 12, 24, or 60 volts. A 2026 peer-reviewed study reached a record 710 mW/cm² peak power density for the chemistry. Read more on the technology page.

04Closing the loopSixty percent of cans are buried.

About 1.3 GWh of energy is buried every year in cans alone.

Sixty percent of aluminum cans in the United States end up in a landfill, and the energy that went into smelting them goes with them. Our generators pull that energy back out as electricity, and the aluminum trihydroxide that remains is sold into secondary markets. The loop has four stops.

  1. Collect

    Cans, manufacturing scrap, and post-consumer aluminum from partners and recyclers.

  2. Convert

    Aluminum is prepared as plate fuel and loaded into the generator.

  3. Generate

    Electricity is produced on site. No grid, no emissions, no noise.

  4. Recover

    Aluminum trihydroxide is collected and sold, and the cycle starts again.

Aluminum.
Air.
Electricity.

An aluminum-air cell generates electricity from the reaction between aluminum and oxygen in ambient air. It is one of the highest energy-density chemistries known, it is lightweight, and it is mechanically simple.

Instead of recharging, the cell is refueled. Spent aluminum plates are swapped for fresh ones, as fast as filling a tank. Aluminum is abundant and fully recyclable, so the fuel supply is the recycling stream.

01How it worksThe cell, in cross-section.

Aluminum oxidizes at the anode and gives up electrons. Oxygen from the air is reduced at the cathode and takes them back. The electrolyte carries hydroxide between the two, and the electrons go through your load.

Systems operate at 1.0–1.2 V per cell and are connected in series to reach 12, 24, or 60 volts depending on the application. Refueling is mechanical, so the customer sees zero downtime. The aluminum trihydroxide produced during operation is recovered and sold to secondary markets, which turns the waste stream into revenue.

02Proven performanceA record for power density.

From 64 to 710 mW/cm².

Peer-reviewed research took a baseline aluminum-air cell to a peak power density of 710 mW/cm², the highest reported for the chemistry on an anode-area basis, by managing the aluminum product film at the anode.

Peak power density on an aluminum-anode-area basis. Prior best reported value per the study's literature survey. Source: Griffith et al., ECS Advances, 2026, 5, 010504 (CC BY 4.0).

03RefuelingRefueled, not recharged.

A lithium battery has to sit on a charger. A diesel generator has to be topped off from a truck. An aluminum-air generator is refueled by removing the spent plates and inserting fresh ones. The exchange is mechanical and takes minutes.

Because the fuel is a solid, it stores indefinitely, ships without hazmat handling, and does not degrade on the shelf. Standby systems stay ready for years without maintenance cycles.

01The problemEnergy buried with the cans.

Sixty percent of aluminum cans end up in a landfill.

Aluminum is one of the most energy-intensive materials to make and one of the easiest to recycle, yet most cans are thrown away. We estimate roughly 1.3 GWh of energy is landfilled each year in cans alone. Energy that could power communities, businesses, and critical infrastructure.

We imagine a future where energy is generated from cans and waste products. Instead of discarding them, we convert them into a low-cost fuel for our energy generation systems, turning a disposal problem into a distributed energy asset.

02Circular economyThe technology sits at the center.

We convert consumer and post-consumer aluminum into energy through electrochemical extraction. The aluminum trihydroxide generated during operation is recovered and sold to secondary markets, creating a second revenue stream.

This is energy independence built on a material that already exists in abundance: in landfills, recycling bins, and on factory floors around the world.

03The loopFour stops, then again.

  1. Collect

    We gather cans, manufacturing scrap, and post-consumer waste from partners and recyclers.

  2. Convert

    Aluminum is prepared as plate fuel and loaded into our electrochemical generation systems.

  3. Generate

    Clean, uninterrupted electricity is produced on site. No grid, no emissions, no noise.

  4. Recover

    Aluminum trihydroxide is collected and sold to secondary markets, completing the loop.
